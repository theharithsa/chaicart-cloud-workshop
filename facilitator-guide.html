The ChaiCart
Cloud Workshop

A two-day, game-based workshop for 120 engineering students (5th and 7th semester) in 24 teams of five. On Day 1 they found a cloud startup; on Day 2 they keep it alive through outages, bill shocks and a murder mystery.

How the workshop works

The story

Every team is the founding team of ChaiCart, a 10-minute chai and snack delivery startup. Each topic on the agenda becomes a decision the startup has to make:

  • Day 1, Build: choose a service model, a deployment model, a cloud provider and an architecture.
  • Day 2, Survive: launch day goes wrong. Teams investigate an outage, manage reliability, control costs, connect business systems and pitch at Demo Day.

Refer back to ChaiCart in every explanation: “So what would this mean for ChaiCart?” The story is what makes the concepts stick.

The team → region structure mirrors real cloud design (availability zones inside regions) and lets you run 24 teams without the day dragging: pitches, judging and marking happen inside regions in parallel, and only the region winners come to the main stage.

Mix the semesters

Put 5th- and 7th-semester students in every team (ideally 2–3 of each). Seniors bring project and internship experience; juniors bring energy. Pre-assign teams on a list and project it as students arrive.

Cloud Credits

Each team starts with 1,000 credits when its Live session is created. The official scores are stored in the ChaiCart Live credit ledger. Project its Screen → Leaderboard at transitions. Captains approve reviews and can apply them once within their region; facilitators can also apply them, and dedicated panels score quizzes and games. leaderboard.html is a legacy practice tool, not the workshop ledger.

Your rhythm for every block

  1. Hook (1–2 min): a question, a story or a surprise.
  2. Concept (5–10 min): the analogy slide first, then the technical slide.
  3. Activity (10–30 min): teams play; captains circulate in their regions.
  4. Debrief (5 min): ask the debrief questions before revealing answers.
  5. Score and transition: captains save reviews digitally; captains or the facilitator apply approved credits; dedicated scoring panels score automatic activities, then reads the transition line.

Three networking icebreakers

Day 1 opening: LinkedIn (slide 6, 7 min). Day 1 after lunch: GitHub (slide 26, 7 min). Day 2 opening: X (slide 4, 5 min). These fit within the existing session blocks; keep introductions and debriefs brief.

Students create or improve their profiles, connect with peers, and use the presenter’s QR codes. LinkedIn: request connections with peers and Vishruth. GitHub: follow peers and theharithsa. X: launch a learning circle in the opening, then continue sharing photos and takeaways throughout Day 2; posting is optional. Use #ChaiCartCloudWorkshop on workshop takeaways and photos so everyone can find them through the hashtag search. Existing accounts count.

Scoring: +100 Cloud Credits per team per activity, once only; maximum +300 over both days. Every teammate completes the activity, and the COO reports completion to the captain. Existing accounts, connections and follows count; students improve their profile and make a useful peer introduction. Do not collect passwords, OTPs, profile screenshots or lists of personal handles. If account setup is delayed, students draft their profile and finish during a break; award the activity once completion is reported.

Logistics

Room, regions and captains

Room layout

  • 24 tables of 5, grouped into four quadrants, one per region, with a clear aisle between quadrants (leave space for volunteer demonstrations).
  • A stage area at the front with space for 7 people in a line (Human Kitchen, Follow the Order).
  • Space for small group discussion during the digital gallery rounds.
  • Project the team map and tell each table its team name and join code.
  • Projector plus two microphones: one for you, one handheld for volunteers and pitches. In a room of 120 a mic is not optional.

People you need

RoleWhoDoes
Lead facilitatorYouRuns the slides, explains, debriefs, keeps time.
Region captains ×4Faculty members or senior volunteersRun region rounds, review digital answers, and submit approved reviews. Share the captain briefing and digital review reference.
ScorekeeperOne volunteer with a laptopHelps verify reviews and project the Live leaderboard; credit application requires an authorized facilitator account.
Tech helper (optional)A volunteer or lab assistantKeeps Azure, Dynatrace and the web app running; handles the second screen.
Each captain account covers one region. If you have only two helpers, assign them one region each and use the facilitator console to review the other two regions and apply scores. Without any helpers, the region rounds still work: teams vote, and region results are read out by one elected CEO per region.

The region-round pattern

Used for Shark Tank, Architecture Lego, the postmortem and Demo Day:

  1. All 24 teams do the activity at their tables.
  2. Inside each region, teams present briefly to each other (60–90 seconds each) while the captain times.
  3. The region votes (each team one vote, not for itself) or the captain picks the best.
  4. The four region winners present on the main stage. You and the captains choose the overall winner.

This keeps all 24 teams active, takes about the same time as 4 presentations, and gives every region a moment on stage.

Before the workshop

Preparation checklist

One week before

  • Get the student list and assign 24 teams of 5, mixing 5th and 7th semester. Assign 6 teams to each region.
  • Confirm with the organiser: hall for 120 with movable tables, projector, 2 microphones, wall space, Wi-Fi.
  • Confirm 4 region captains and a scorekeeper. Send them the captain briefing.
  • Prepare reliable Wi-Fi, phone charging, two microphones, projector, four captain accounts and prizes for the closing awards.
  • Run through both decks once, out loud, with a timer. Rehearse the Azure and Dynatrace demos.

Paperless student and captain setup

ChaiCart Live is the student workspace. This is separate from the Azure storefront used for cloud demos.

  1. Open Console, sign in as a facilitator, create or select the session and set its workshop date. Setup displays the 24 team join codes.
  2. Register four captain Google emails with one region each in Setup. Captains open Captain dashboard; they can review only their six teams.
  3. Students sign in with Google, enter the session and team code, and claim one of five unique roles. The COO submits shared answers; everyone completes their own surveys, networking and 90-day plan.
  4. Run selects and opens the live activity. Workshop provides Bingo clues, frozen designs, digital stations, factory rounds, review approval, X shortlists and certificate release.
  5. Keep Networking open across the day for optional X posts using #ChaiCartCloudWorkshop. Ask permission before photographing people. Judge learning, creativity and workshop spirit, not likes. Record the individual award before releasing certificates.

No printed cards, worksheets, envelopes, dice or planes are required. Human Kitchen and Follow the Order still use volunteers, with digital station cards and timestamped handoffs. Keep Wi-Fi, chargers, a projector and microphones ready. If connectivity fails, continue the discussion verbally, pause submissions and record corrections after service returns.

Operating and recovery instructions: ChaiCart Live README. Legacy printable packs remain optional reference material.

Awards at Demo Day
