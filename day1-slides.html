The analogy

🏨

CHAPTER 4

Virtualization &
Containerization

Houses, apartments and tiffin boxes

The analogy

🏠 → 🏢 → 🍱

🏠

Bare metal = Independent house

One family (app) per plot. Lots of wasted space. Slow to build.

🏢

Virtual Machine = Apartment

One building (server), many flats. Each flat has its own kitchen (own OS). The builder's structure = hypervisor.

🍱

Container = Tiffin box

Everyone shares one big kitchen (host OS kernel), but each meal is sealed and portable. Light, fast, identical everywhere.

CHAPTER 6

Cloud-Native &
Microservices

One chef or a whole kitchen?

Monolith vs microservices

👨‍🍳 One super-chef vs 🧑‍🍳🧑‍🍳🧑‍🍳 a station kitchen

Monolith

  • One big app, one codebase, one deployment
  • Simple to start ✅
  • Chef falls sick → restaurant closes ❌
  • Want more dosas? Clone the whole chef ❌

Microservices

  • Small services: order, payment, kitchen, delivery, notification
  • Each team deploys independently ✅
  • Scale only the busy station ✅
  • But: many network calls → harder to debug ⚠️

☁️ Cloud-native = containers + microservices + APIs + CI/CD + DevOps + automation, built to run on elastic cloud.

ACTIVITY · The Human Kitchen · 20 min · Volunteers' teams +20

Become microservices

Six volunteers from six different teams come to the front. Everyone else counts, times and cheers.

Round 1 — Monolith (3 min)

ONE volunteer processes every order ticket alone: confirm Order, Payment, Kitchen, Rider and SMS steps in the app. The room counts orders done.

Round 2 — Microservices (3 min)

FIVE volunteers, one station card each. Tickets flow along the line. Each station writes its time on the ticket.

Round 3 — Chaos (3 min)

Mid-round, I tap the Payment person and they freeze. What happens to orders? Who notices? How fast?

Debrief

What did the kitchen teach us?

  • Throughput went up — parallelism & independent scaling
  • One frozen station blocked everything → need timeouts, retries, fallbacks
  • Nobody knew where the order was stuck… until we read the timestamps on the digital token

🔍 That ticket was a distributed trace!

Each station = a span. The ticket number = a trace ID. Hold that thought for the end of today.

CHAPTER 8

Cloud Observability
& Monitoring — a first look

The doctor analogy 🩺

How do you know your app is healthy?

📈

Metrics = Vital signs

Pulse, BP, temperature → CPU, response time, error rate, orders/min. Numbers over time.

📓

Logs = Patient's diary

“09:02 felt dizzy after lunch” → 09:02 ERROR payment timeout. Detailed events.

🩻

Traces = MRI scan

Follows one request through every organ (service) and shows exactly where it got stuck.

Monitoring tells you that something is wrong. Observability lets you ask why — even for problems you never predicted.
